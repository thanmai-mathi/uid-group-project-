🌎 Shaping Earth’s Continents

Earth's crust broke into tectonic plates. A continent is any of several large terrestrial geographical regions. These plates moved slowly and formed continents. All continents were once joined as Pangaea. Continents are formed through a billions-of-years-long process driven by plate tectonics, where internal heat causes molten rock to rise, cool, and create lighter, buoyant continental crust that floats above the denser oceanic crust. Pangaea included the landmasses that are now recognized as the seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia, Europe, North America, and South America.
